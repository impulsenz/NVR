# What's new in GAZE

## 1.7.0 — November 2026
- **Heat map is now its own page** (the *Heat map* tab), a function rather than a setting, and no longer an overlay in the camera's Motion settings. Review every camera at once, over the last 24 hours, 7 or 30 days, for: **Motion** (everything that moved, even where movement is already ignored), **Motion nothing recognised** (movement in which the AI never found anything: branches, flags, shadows, reflections: the places to paint as ignore areas, with an *Ignore areas…* button per camera that opens that camera's ignore-area painting), **Everything the AI sees**, **People**, **Vehicles**, **Animals**, **Other objects** and **any one kind** the AI has seen (cat, backpack, chair …). *Bigger* shows one camera large; your existing ignore areas are drawn on top (switch off with *Show ignore areas*).
- **Smoother heat map.** It is blurred and scaled up smoothly with a soft colour ramp scaled so one very busy spot doesn't wash out the rest, instead of coloured blocks. It was also short of data: it only had one picture per alert. GAZE now **logs every look of the motion detector and of the AI** (all of it, whether or not it alerts or is recorded; small files in `nvr-data/heat/`, kept 30 days), so it fills in properly after a few days. Until then, older alerts' pictures stand in, and the page says when there is not much data yet.
- **Floor plan: resizable camera icons.** *Cameras* tab → *Icon size* makes all the camera icons bigger or smaller (30% to 400%), and each camera has its own *Icon size* too. The icons are sized in proportion to the map, so they grow when you zoom in (they used to stay one screen size).
- **Daily summary: much faster and busier.** Objects now play at 2× real time by default (*Settings → Daily summary → Speed*: as it happened, 2×, 3×, 4×, 6×), and objects that were in different parts of the picture at different times now **play at the same time**, however far apart they really were, instead of one after another. The default length is 60 s (it was 120 s; summaries you already set keep their length). A very busy day lets objects overlap a little and then plays faster to fit.
- **Daily summary: smoother.** The footage is looked at 6 times a second (was 4) and the video shows 24 pictures a second (was 12), with each object moved smoothly between the moments it was seen instead of stepping. Edges fade over a few pixels instead of cutting hard, objects are lightened or darkened to suit the empty scene they are shown on (a person seen at night no longer looks like a dark patch on a day scene), and the box around each is drawn faintly.
- **Daily summary: pause for the footage.** Pause the video (or jump to a point) and a link appears next to each object on screen: *person 02:03:41 · open footage ↗* opens the real recording at that moment.

## 1.6.0 — November 2026
- **Daily summary shows only what the AI recognised, and you choose what.** The 1.5.0 *Day summary* showed any movement; it now shows only objects the AI names (people, vehicles, dogs, cats, birds, and any of the other kinds the AI knows), each boxed and labelled with its name and the clock time it was there (people green, vehicles blue, animals amber). Shadows, swaying plants, rain and anything the AI does not name are left out. **It works for kinds you never get alerts for or record as alerts**: the AI looks at the footage again for the summary, so a cat or a delivery van shows even if nothing was ever alerted. Pick the kinds in *Settings → Daily summary*.
- **Make summaries for all cameras at once**, for today so far, yesterday, the last 24 / 12 / 8 hours or any day (*Daily summary* button on the Alerts page). They are made one after another in a queue, with progress for each.
- **A schedule**: *Settings → Daily summary* → make a summary every day at a time you choose (for example 06:30 to have it when you wake up, 17:30 for when you get home), covering the hours before that time, for the cameras you choose. It is waiting on the Alerts page; if GAZE was off then it is made when it starts (up to 6 hours late). Off by default.
- **Summaries live on the Alerts page in their own section**, with a dashed frame, a *DAILY SUMMARY* badge, a note that they are not alerts, and a *made automatically* tag on scheduled ones. Click a card to watch; click an object (or its time) to open the real footage at that moment. They are kept for the number of days you set (30 by default) and can be deleted from the player.
- The *Day summary* button moved from Recordings to the Alerts page and is now called *Daily summary*. **Tried on made-up footage and the real AI model on it, not yet on real photographs or a real camera: please tell us how it does.**

## 1.5.0 — November 2026
- **Only an administrator can restart GAZE.** The *Restart GAZE* button in the 👤 menu was available to every signed-in account (operators and viewers); it is now shown to administrators only and the server refuses the request from anyone else.
- **Day summary** (Recordings → 🎬 *Day summary*): one camera's whole day in a short video. The empty scene stays still and everything that moved that day is laid over it, shifted in time so people and cars from different hours appear together, each with a box and the clock time it was really there. **Click an object** (or its time in the list) and the real footage opens at that moment. Choose the camera, the day, the length (1, 2 or 5 minutes) and how sensitive it is; if a very busy day won't fit, objects are allowed to overlap a little and then played faster (it says so). It is made on request, one at a time, at low priority (it waits while the computer is busy), from the light timeline copy where there is one, else the recordings (deep storage included), takes minutes (measured on a 4-core test machine, release build, 640×360 footage: 20 minutes of footage in 7 seconds, so about 9 minutes for a whole day; slower on a busy computer or with larger footage), is kept 30 days in `nvr-data/synopsis/`, and can be downloaded. It never touches recordings, alerts or detection, and nothing is changed or deleted. Things that stop moving (a parked car) become part of the scene after a minute, and swaying plants or a flag are not objects. **New, tried on made-up footage only**: please tell us how it does on a real camera.
## 1.4.0 — November 2026
- **Floor plan: pick your property from a satellite map.** *Pick from a satellite map…* (Plan page) opens a map in the page: search an address (or type coordinates), zoom with the mouse wheel or buttons, drag, rotate, and take a snapshot as the plan picture. The scale (metres per pixel) is set from the map automatically. The imagery is loaded by your browser from a provider you choose (Esri, OpenStreetMap, or your own tile address); GAZE itself still downloads no maps. The address search sends the text typed to OpenStreetMap's Nominatim. The snapshot keeps the provider's credit line. **Not tested against the real providers yet** (only a local stand-in).
- **Floor plan: walls.** Draw walls (and fences); each camera's drawn view now stops at them, and a gap is a door. Straight-line and corner-snap help; Ctrl+Z, erase, Esc to finish. Walls are saved in the plan (cleared if the picture changes shape). They only affect the drawn views, not what is tracked.
- **Floor plan: zoom and pan.** Mouse wheel zooms at the pointer; drag to pan; buttons and pinch on touch screens; fit to window.
- **Floor plan: a shorter sidebar.** Tabs (Plan / Cameras / Walls / People) instead of one tall column; the settings are grouped and fold away.
- **Removed: the ten alternative "looks"** (Rail, Dock, Terminal, …). Only the original design remains; Day / Night colours stay. A saved look choice in a browser is simply ignored.

## 1.3.2 — November 2026
- **Recordings: footage in deep storage now plays when you scrub through time.** The player that follows the timeline built its playlist only from files in the camera's own folder, so anything that had moved to deep storage gave an empty playlist: the players stayed black and nothing loaded (the footage was listed, and opening one recording at a time worked). It now finds the files in deep storage. This went back to long before 1.3.

## 1.3.1 — November 2026
- **Recordings: the date picker and the camera picker are no longer drawn under the players.** The filter column on the Recordings page sat on a layer below the video tiles and the timeline, so the calendar was cut off on its right side (and partly hidden behind the camera names in the vertical timeline). This was present in earlier versions too. A check for it is now part of the page smoke test.

## 1.3.0 — November 2026
- **Floor plan** (new **Plan** page): put your cameras on a picture of your home or property (a floor plan, a sketch, or an aerial / satellite view you supply; GAZE never downloads maps), set the scale, place each camera with the way it faces and how wide it sees, and calibrate it by clicking four spots on the ground in its picture and on the map. GAZE can then **follow people**: it joins what each camera sees into walks, joins walks across cameras by timing, distance, walking speed and (weakly) clothing colour into journeys, and draws each journey as a path on the map with a playback slider, how long it took, how far and how fast, and a percentage for how sure each join is. It also shows how many people came, the average visit, which cameras they passed, an hour-by-hour chart, and live dots for people on camera now. Neighbouring cameras can be drawn with their usual walking time. **Fourteen settings fine-tune it**, each explained in the page (minimum confidence, match radius, walk gap, shortest walk, smoothing, longest gap between cameras, camera overlap, fastest speed, extra distance, minimum join score, how much timing / distance / colour count, what to follow, how long walks are kept). It is **off until you switch it on**; it keeps only positions and times (never pictures), for the days you set, and *Delete all walks* removes them. A limited account sees only its own cameras. Without calibration you still get which cameras a person passed, but no path or speed. **New and untested on real cameras: please try it and tell us how the paths and joins look.** Recording and alerts never depend on it.
- **Where alerts happened** (camera → Motion, under *Ignore areas*): shows on the camera's picture where people, vehicles and animals were found in the alerts of the last 24 hours, 7 or 30 days (blue = a few times, orange and red = most often), for everything or one kind. A red patch where nothing matters — a swaying tree, a road — is the place to paint as an ignore area.
- **Daily digest** (Settings → Notifications): one message each morning (phone and/or email) that sums up the last 24 hours — alerts by people, vehicles and animals, who and which plates were recognised, activity after dark, and problems that exist right now (a camera offline, a drive nearly full or failing). Built by counting stored data, no AI. Off by default; *Send a test digest now* shows what it looks like.
- **Phone**: the status bar no longer covers the tab bar at the bottom of the screen.

## 1.2.2 — November 2026
- **Recordings cut short by a power cut or crash**: a file that holds no video at all (only its header, because the power failed within the first second of a new file) is now removed during the repair instead of staying in the list as a recording that can't be played. Files with any video are repaired as before. (Checked with real recordings cut at 60 places and with zero-filled endings.)
- **ffmpeg download (Windows)** is checked against the checksum list its builder publishes with it; a damaged or cut-off download is deleted and not used. If the list can't be read, ffmpeg is used anyway so recording is never held up.
- **Security**: downloaded programs and libraries are now checked against a pinned checksum before they are used — go2rtc, the Piper voice engine and the DirectML runtime (a file that doesn't match is deleted and not used). The sign-in lock-out can no longer be dodged by sending made-up `X-Forwarded-For` addresses through a reverse proxy on the same computer, or by changing address inside an IPv6 /64.
- **Tests** for the recording repair after a power cut (real ffmpeg recordings cut at 60 places, and with zero-filled endings) and for the snooze-link signature (the hash is checked against the RFC 4231 answer).
- **Linux download**: releases now also carry `GAZE-linux-x86_64` (with its checksum). GAZE doesn't update itself on Linux. A Linux check (tests, page smoke test, Docker image build and start) now runs on every change. The Windows release is unaffected (the Linux build is a separate job).

## 1.2.1 — November 2026
- **Page smoke test** for developers (`tools/e2e/smoke.js`): opens every page and settings section in both themes and fails on script errors. No change to GAZE itself.
- **Linux packaging**: a systemd unit (`packaging/gaze.service`), a Dockerfile (`packaging/Dockerfile`) and a guide (`docs/LINUX.md`). The Docker image has not been built yet and there's no Linux download in the releases yet.
- **Sturdier camera access checks** (no visible change): the rule that keeps a camera-limited account off other cameras now lives in one tested function instead of twelve copies.
- **Drive health** (Settings → System): reads every drive's own health report (SMART) through `smartctl` (smartmontools, installed separately) and shows model, size, temperature, age and health. It checks every few hours, and with *Warn me when something's wrong* on, notifies you (phone and/or email) when a drive starts to fail — reallocated or unreadable sectors, a failed SMART status, an NVMe critical warning or nearly-used-up life, overheating — so you can copy the footage off in time. If smartctl isn't installed, or isn't allowed to read the drives (it usually needs administrator / root rights), the page says so. Recording never depends on it.

## 1.2.0 — November 2026
- **Learned states** (Settings → Learned states): teach GAZE what a camera sees from a few examples — garage door open or closed, bin out or in, gate, a light. Make one, then press *Add example* while the scene is in each state; GAZE compares the camera's picture with the examples every few seconds (using the picture model description search already uses; 420 MB downloaded once, nothing leaves the computer, no training) and shows the answer. Optionally choose the piece of the picture that matters, and be notified (phone and/or email) when it changes. The answer is also sent to MQTT, and appears in Home Assistant as a sensor. A change must be seen twice in a row, so a passing shadow doesn't flip it. It never affects recording or alerts.
- **AI on Linux: OpenVINO and CUDA** (Settings → Object recognition → Graphics API). *Automatic* uses CUDA when an NVIDIA card with CUDA 12 and cuDNN 9 is installed, otherwise OpenVINO, which runs on Intel graphics, an Intel NPU, or just the processor (faster than GAZE's built-in engine). The add-on downloads by itself the first time (OpenVINO about 85 MB, CUDA about 250 MB), is checked against a pinned SHA-256 and kept in `nvr-data/runtime/`. Changing it takes a restart. Windows keeps DirectML. Tested: OpenVINO on a processor (same answers as the built-in engine for detection, faces and plates). **Not tested on real hardware: CUDA, Intel graphics, NPU.** If an accelerator can't run a model, that model uses the processor and Settings → Tasks says why.
- **Email notifications** (Settings → Notifications → Email): every notification (alert picture, summary, camera and drive warnings) can also go by email through any SMTP server (STARTTLS, TLS or a plain relay on your network), to one or more addresses, with the picture attached. Works with or without ntfy. The password is kept in the settings file and never sent back to the page. Existing settings files are unchanged (email is off by default).
- **Sturdier internals** (no visible change): a fault in one part of GAZE can no longer lock up other parts through a poisoned lock (`src/sync.rs`); a test now fails if a new camera-specific web address is added without an access decision.

## 1.1.10 — November 2026
- **Faster page loads**: the page, its script and styles are compressed and remembered by the browser: the first load is about 70 % smaller (900 KB → 260 KB) and a reload downloads almost nothing.
- **Security**: the Evidence Locker no longer lets an account that can't see a camera change or delete cases and items holding that camera's footage; the streaming (HLS) addresses are covered by the central camera check; a camera's address must be a streaming address (rtsp://, rtsps://, http(s)://, rtmp(s)://, srt://, rtp://, udp://, tcp://) — addresses that read files or run programs are refused when entered (cameras already saved are not affected).
- Audit report and project documentation (`docs/AUDIT-2026-11.md`, architecture map, technical-debt register, configuration inventory, security checklist, tooling), rules for future development (`CLAUDE.md`), and a pull-request quality workflow (tests, lints, Windows compile check).

## 1.1.9 — November 2026
- **Watchdog reopens GAZE however it stopped**: crashed, hung, ended in Task Manager or closed with Quit. (Before, it left GAZE closed after Quit. That's now an opt-in setting: “Leave GAZE closed when I quit it myself”.)
- **The watchdog is its own Windows service** (Settings → Watchdog → “As its own Windows service”): starts with the computer, before anyone signs in, and Windows restarts the watchdog itself if it ever stops. It updates itself as a service too.
- **The watchdog has its own icon** next to the clock (the GAZE eye with a green tick): status at a glance; menu to open GAZE, restart it, pause watching, open the log.
- **Day theme**: the timelines (Recordings and the camera view, horizontal and vertical), the server-load graph and the sign-in card are now light on the day theme (they were fixed dark); night theme unchanged.

## 1.1.8 — November 2026

It plays straight from deep storage (read there, not copied back), shows in the Alerts / Recordings list, plays from alerts, can be exported, and deleting it deletes it from there.
- **Vertical timeline**: the recording-now bar looks like the bottom timeline's (its colour with a moving striped end); camera names slant up to the right so they read the right way up, with the recording dot at the top.
- **Folders are named as you call the camera**: older cameras that record into a folder named after their id are renamed to their name when GAZE starts (instant, on the same drive), and a camera's folder in deep storage follows when you rename the camera.

## 1.1.7 — November 2026

## 1.1.6 — November 2026
- **GAZE Watchdog** (optional, its own program `GAZE-Watchdog.exe`): watches GAZE and opens it again if it crashes or stops answering. It starts with Windows — when someone signs in, or when the computer starts — so after a restart it opens GAZE too. Install, set up, monitor and update it from the browser: **Settings → Watchdog** (status, last check, restarts, log, check interval, restarts allowed before it waits, how GAZE is opened — program or Windows service). It has its own updates (it can install them by itself, or you update it from the same page). If you quit GAZE yourself, the watchdog leaves it closed.

## 1.1.5 — November 2026
- **Deep storage** (Settings → Recording & storage, redesigned): footage stays on the fast local disk for as long as you say (24 hours by default), then moves to a NAS or big drive — copied, checked (size and checksum) and only then taken off the local disk. Deep storage has its own keep time (days) and size limit; footage in an Evidence Locker case is never deleted from it.
- Set it for all cameras, or give a camera its own times, or keep it on the local disk. Each camera shows what's on the local disk, what's in deep storage and what's waiting to move; Browse plays or downloads footage from deep storage.
- Moves show under System → Tasks (files left, size, speed) and can be paused; **Move now** and **Test the folder** buttons; a picture across the top shows the path footage takes.
- With deep storage on, the local disk's age limit is replaced by the move; the disk-space limits (maximum storage, keep free) still apply.
- The timeline shows what is on the local disk; older footage is in deep storage. (This replaces the "data retention" option of Backup added in 1.1.2.)

## 1.1.1 — November 2026
- **go2rtc with TP-Link Tapo (and similar) cameras**: go2rtc no longer asks cameras for their two-way audio channel, which left some cameras (Tapo among them) with no picture ("could not find codec parameters"). GAZE talks through cameras itself as before.
- **A camera that gets no picture through go2rtc connects straight to the camera** after two tries (for 10 minutes, then tries go2rtc again), so it keeps recording. What go2rtc said about it is written to Settings → Logs.

## 1.1.0 — November 2026
- **Evidence Locker** (new Evidence page): keep alerts, clips and pictures in cases. Everything added is copied into the case and protected — it can't be deleted from Recordings, Alerts, Delete footage or by the automatic clean-up until it's unprotected in its case.
- **Live view**: the opened camera fills its box edge to edge with its controls underneath; choose all cameras, a group or any cameras from one menu; full screen shows the same layout as the Live page; zoom into any picture with the mouse wheel; no more full-screen button on each picture.
- **Pan / tilt / zoom**: ＋ adds a preset, a bin deletes one, and the controls stay on screen when Track or Roam opens.
- **Recordings and alerts**: opens on the stream that's recorded all the time; LIVE shows instead of PLAYBACK while watching live; Back after opening an alert returns to exactly where you were; Download clip next to Full alert; recognized people are named on alert pictures.
- **Ignore pets** (camera → AI): no alerts for dogs and cats on their own; a person seen with them still alerts.
- **System**: a hardware bar along the bottom (network, CPU, GPU, memory, free space) and a drawer with the same in full screen; green / yellow / red load colours; alerts, motion, cars and people per hour for each camera; the page uses the whole width of wide screens.
- **Lighter live video**: one stream per camera shared by everyone watching, paused when the tab is hidden.
- **go2rtc** can be chosen as the streaming engine (Settings → System, to try out); FFmpeg stays the default.
- **Backups to a folder or NAS are checked** (size and checksum) before they count.
- Profiles are gone (cameras keep their settings); Save and Save and close when editing a camera; GAZE opens at its https address.
